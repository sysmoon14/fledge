Fledge documentation

Fledge roots a Nest Learning Thermostat (3rd generation) over USB, runs it on your own network without Google's cloud, and puts Home Assistant on its screen.

Read this first. Fledge has been tested on exactly one thermostat: a 3rd generation Nest Learning Thermostat on Nest firmware 6.4-8, a European model with a Heat Link driving heating and hot water, set up with MQTT and Home Assistant. On anything else, you are the first.

It rewrites the flash of the device that runs your heating. A failed run can leave the thermostat unbootable. Take the backup the app offers, and have another way to heat the house while you try it. Fledge comes without any warranty.

Two parts

Fledge comes in two pieces, released separately, that talk to each other only over your MQTT broker:

The first part alone gives you a local, cloud-free thermostat in Home Assistant. The second puts your home on its screen.

How it works

Nest's own app, nlclient, keeps running the thermostat: the heating, the schedule, the learning and the safety limits. Fledge adds nestlocal beside it. nestlocal stands in for Google's cloud on the thermostat itself and relays everything to MQTT, Matter or Zigbee on your network instead. Google's servers can be blocked outright.

When you walk up, nlclient hands its display and ring to nestlocal, which draws your Home Assistant devices as cards at 60 frames a second, with the Nest's own clicks. The thermostat's own card and its everyday Settings are drawn the same way; nestlocal sets the thermostat through the same settings Home Assistant uses. Nest's own screens come back for its dial (if you choose it) and its full menus.

What you need

  • A Nest Learning Thermostat, 3rd generation. The 1st and 2nd generation and the Nest Thermostat E aren't supported; for those, use unfr0zen.
  • A micro-USB cable that carries data, and a Mac, Windows or Linux computer.
  • Home Assistant 2026.3 or later with an MQTT broker (the Mosquitto add-on is fine) that the thermostat can reach over Wi-Fi. The network doesn't need internet access.
